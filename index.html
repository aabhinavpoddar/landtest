Regeneration of Unplanned Private Land

A practical overview of the policy framework and approval pathway described in the Master Plan for Delhi–2047 (MPD-2047), with emphasis on private landowners and unplanned settlements.

Chapter 4.6 · pages 120–137 (printed page numbers)Gazette notification: 20 August 2026Plain-language summary
Important scope note: “Regeneration of unplanned areas” is not one single route for every private plot. MPD-2047 treats unauthorised colonies, urban villages, slum/JJ clusters, non-conforming industrial areas and godown clusters differently. Eligibility, land status, access, land use, and the relevant authority’s regulations must be checked for the particular site.

1. What the Master Plan is trying to achieve

The Plan describes unplanned/unauthorised areas as often having poor building conditions, inadequate physical and social infrastructure, and difficult access—especially for emergency services. Regeneration is intended to improve safety, infrastructure and quality of life while taking account of existing density, built form and land-ownership issues.

Area-wide planningPlan roads, access, services, open spaces and community facilities together rather than treating every plot in isolation.
Owner participationPrivate owners may coordinate through a Developer Entity (DE), residents’ welfare associations (RWAs), owners’ associations or an authorised representative, where the relevant policy permits.
Regulatory approvalsLayout/service plans and individual building plans are separate approvals where the policy specifies both stages.
Infrastructure fundingApplicable regeneration and infrastructure charges may be payable; the Plan describes a dedicated, ring-fenced Regeneration Fund for UC regeneration charges.

Source: MPD-2047, Chapter 4.6 “Regeneration of Un-planned Areas”, introductory text and clauses 4.6.1–4.6.6.

2. Typical process for a private landowner

The steps below synthesise the relevant provisions. The exact sequence and documents depend on which category the land falls under; this is not a substitute for the applicable regulations or a formal approval checklist.

Identify the category
Confirm whether the site is in an eligible UC, urban village, notified industrial area or godown cluster.
Check exclusions
Check environmental sensitivity, Zone O, heritage/ASI/NMA controls and other stated exclusions.
Coordinate owners
Where a scheme is used, assemble participating owners and create the legally enforceable agreement/entity required by that policy.
Prepare area plan
Prepare the applicable layout/services plan, Area Improvement Plan or Road Network Plan (RNP) with the relevant authority.
Secure plan approval
Submit to the concerned authority/local body; follow the specific approval route for the category.
Pay applicable charges
Pay notified regeneration, infrastructure, use-conversion and other applicable charges.
Obtain building sanction
Apply for individual building-plan approval under the applicable regulations/UBBL provisions.
Implement improvements
Provide access, road-widening land where required, services, safety measures and public amenities in accordance with the approved plan.
Do not assume automatic regularisation. The Master Plan sets a framework. It does not say that every existing structure or every privately owned plot is automatically regularised or approved merely because it lies in an unplanned area.

3. Unauthorised Colonies (UCs): regeneration scheme route

This section is particularly relevant to eligible private residential plots in unauthorised colonies. MPD-2047 distinguishes regularisation from a regeneration scheme.

Who may prepare a scheme?

  • A Developer Entity (DE), an RWA, or an authorised lead representing a part of a UC or a cluster of UCs.
  • The group must act through a legally enforceable agreement.
  • The scheme may cover an entire UC, part of a UC, or a cluster of adjoining UCs, subject to the policy and exclusions.

FAR = Floor Area Ratio. Amenities FAR is an incentive over and above the scheme FAR for eligible public/semi-public facilities, subject to level-specific restrictions. The plan states that up to 15% of the scheme FAR may be used for local facilities, with only 5% for local-level commercial use.

Roads, open space, parking and charges

  • Setbacks depend on road ROW: 2 m on each side for roads of 9 m or more; 3 m where ROW is above 7.5 m and below 9 m; 4.5 m where ROW is above 3 m and below 7.5 m; and 6 m where ROW is up to 3 m or adjoining existing built-up areas. Setbacks must remain clear for possible road widening/construction.
  • Public space: Level 1 and 2 schemes provide 10% as a single plot. Levels 3 and 4 provide 10% as open space for public purpose, including at least one plot of 500 sq m (Level 3) or 1,000 sq m (Level 4).
  • Parking: 1 Equivalent Car Space (ECS) per 100 sq m, irrespective of use premise.
  • Land for public/semi-public facilities must be provided as a separate consolidated plot with at least 9 m ROW access and handed over to the local body, as prescribed.
  • Base FAR 350 is subject to requisite notified charges. The DE/RWA/authorised lead pays regeneration and applicable infrastructure charges after scheme approval; the charges are to be ring-fenced through an escrow account for the dedicated Regeneration Fund.
  • Level 4 schemes may be implemented in smaller blocks of at least 3,000 sq m after approval of the overall scheme.
Regularisation versus regeneration: Regularisation of UCs is governed by the notified NCT of Delhi (Recognition of Property Rights of Residents in Unauthorised Colonies) Regulations, 2019 and subsequent amendments. A UC regeneration scheme is a separate route for area planning and redevelopment. Do not treat one as a replacement for the other.

4. Urban villages and Lal Dora areas

For urban villages, MPD-2047 provides for an Area Improvement Plan prepared by the concerned local body in consultation with residents, RWAs and other stakeholders. It may cover the entire village or a defined precinct bounded by roads, parks, railway corridors, drains or natural features.

  • The plan is to include an RNP addressing congestion and widening roads up to a minimum ROW of 7.5 m.
  • Plots abutting roads earmarked for widening must provide land at reconstruction; the permissible FAR may be used on the remaining plot.
  • Gram Sabha land and community-held/shared land may be identified for local public/semi-public facilities, shared parking, public spaces, utilities, recycling and evacuation areas.
  • Water bodies, heritage assets, local greens and orchards are to be considered in area improvement.
  • Individual plotted-housing building-plan approvals may be provided without insisting on an approved village layout plan, as provided by the regulations.
  • Regeneration schemes in urban villages are permitted under the norms prescribed for UC regeneration schemes.

The gross land use for urban villages is considered Residential. The Plan allows specified local-level facilities and compatible economic activities subject to road access, FAR and statutory clearances. Check the exact clause and current regulations before relying on any use permission.

5. Non-conforming industrial areas

For notified areas with a concentration of industrial activity (more than 70% of plots with industrial activity), the concerned local bodies/agencies, in consultation with industry-owner associations, prepare and notify an area-level RNP.

  • The RNP records existing roads and widths, road widening, and access from an existing road with minimum 18 m ROW.
  • After the RNP/layout plan is approved, owners of regularised plots may choose plot-level building-plan approval on existing plots or join together as a DE for a regeneration scheme.
  • At plot level, owners must provide land for road widening as prescribed. The Plan permits use of the FAR of the original plot on the remaining plot after surrender, subject to conditions.
  • Plot-level norms stated in this clause include FAR 200, with up to 10% of permissible FAR for administrative office/retail, and applicable parking, safety, service and environmental requirements.
  • For regeneration, applicable provisions for regeneration of planned industrial areas apply; all applicable charges are payable.

The Plan lists multiple excluded areas where industries are not permitted. Confirm that the parcel is within a notified eligible industrial area and not within an exclusion.

6. Non-conforming godown clusters

The Plan provides a distinct route for identified godown clusters. The relevant authority identifies/delineates eligible clusters in consultation with owners/associations.

  • A cluster must be contiguous, at least 2 hectares, existing before 21 June 2018, have more than 55% of plots operating as godowns, and have direct access from a road with at least 30 m ROW.
  • The concerned agencies prepare and approve an RNP with owners’ associations. It must provide direct cluster access from a 30 m ROW road, a network of roads at least 9 m ROW throughout, and specific roads widened to at least 12 m.
  • The approved RNP serves as the cluster’s approved layout plan. Owners may seek building-plan approval on existing plots or undertake a regeneration scheme.
  • A regeneration scheme for all or part of a cluster must cover at least 2 hectares. It requires direct access from an 18 m road in the RNP; an entire-cluster scheme must access a 30 m ROW road.
  • Road-widening land and public areas must be handled as specified in the approved RNP and scheme. Applicable use-conversion and other charges must be paid.
Indicative warehousing scheme norms: maximum FAR 100; maximum ground coverage 70% on individual plots; up to 25% of scheme-level permissible FAR for ancillary uses such as offices, wholesale trade and/or big-box retail; up to 10% of scheme area for common facilities; parking at 2 ECS per 100 sq m FAR. All remain subject to applicable Master Plan norms and safety/environmental requirements.

7. Practical checklist for a private owner or owner group

  1. Establish the land category and boundaries: obtain the relevant revenue/colony/cluster records and confirm the site’s planning-zone status.
  2. Check ownership and authority: collect title/ownership or rights documents, plot details, existing sanctioned plans (if any), and confirm who can legally represent the participating owners.
  3. Check exclusions: confirm whether the site is affected by Zone O, environmental restrictions, Ridge/forest, water bodies, protected monuments/heritage controls, roads or other listed exclusions.
  4. Measure area and access: verify contiguous scheme area, existing road widths/ROW, access to the required wider road, and potential road-widening contributions.
  5. Choose the correct pathway: UC regeneration scheme, urban-village Area Improvement Plan, industrial-area RNP/plot approval or regeneration, or godown-cluster RNP/scheme.
  6. Coordinate with the right authority: ask the concerned local body/DDA/agency which regulations and application route are currently in force for the parcel.
  7. Prepare technical plans: engage qualified planning/architecture and engineering professionals for layout, services, road network, parking, fire access, utilities, drainage and public amenities.
  8. Get approvals and cost estimates in writing: confirm charges, required NOCs, handover/surrender conditions, and the separate building-plan sanction requirements before committing to construction.

This checklist is a practical synthesis of the Plan, not an official list of mandatory application documents.

8. Important cautions and source references

  • MPD-2047 states that regulations for regeneration of unplanned areas, wherever required, shall be prepared after notification of the Plan. Therefore, operational procedures, forms, charges and current implementation details should be verified with the concerned authority.
  • Eligibility under one category does not establish ownership, guarantee regularisation, or remove other statutory approvals.
  • Land in environmentally sensitive areas, Zone O, areas regulated by NMA/ASI, Civil Lines Bungalow Area and Lutyens’ Bungalow Zone is excluded from the general applicability of Chapter 4.6; the Plan says such areas follow the relevant agency guidelines/regulations.
  • Numerical norms shown here are transcribed/summarised from the document and should be checked against the exact notified text and subsequent amendments before a property or investment decision.
